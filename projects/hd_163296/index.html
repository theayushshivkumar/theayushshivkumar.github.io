Multi-epoch Spectroscopic Study of the Protoplanetary Disk HD 163296

Master's Thesis • IISER Thiruvananthapuram & Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) (06/2025 – 05/2026); continuing as an independent research project)

Project Supervisor: Dr. Manash Samal (PRL)

Overview

My master’s thesis investigated the physical and chemical evolution of the inner protoplanetary disk around the Herbig Ae star HD 163296. By combining multi-epoch observations spanning the optical to the mid-infrared, I explored the connection between accretion variability, molecular gas properties, and short-timescale photometric variability in the terrestrial planet-forming region of the disk.

Scientific Motivation

The inner regions of protoplanetary disks are where terrestrial planets are expected to form. Despite its importance, this region remains difficult to spatially resolve. Spectroscopic diagnostics and photometric variability studies therefore provide valuable indirect probes of the physical conditions, chemistry, and gas dynamics within the inner disk. HD 163296 is an ideal laboratory owing to its rich molecular spectrum, extensive multi-wavelength observations, and the unconfirmed existence of multiple planetary companions.

Observational Data

This project combined archival observations from multiple facilities:

  • JWST/MIRI MRS
  • VLT/X-shooter
  • CFHT/ESPaDOnS
  • Keck/NIRSPEC
  • TESS

Together, these datasets enabled a multi-wavelength and multi-epoch investigation of both the gas chemistry and variability of the inner disk.

Techniques

Throughout this project, I worked extensively with

  • Multi-epoch spectroscopy
  • Gaussian emission-line fitting
  • Equivalent width measurements
  • Accretion rate estimation
  • Lomb–Scargle and Phase Dispersion Minimisation techniques to identify periodic signals from TESS light curves.
  • Continuous Wavelet Transform analysis
  • LTE slab modelling of H₂O emission
  • CO rovibrational slab modelling
  • Python-based astronomical data analysis

Selected Results

  • Measured mass accretion rates of the order 10⁻⁷ M⊙ yr⁻¹, typical of actively accreting Herbig Ae stars.
  • Detected quasi-periodic photometric variability with a dominant period near 0.8 days, possibly regulated by rotational modulation and accretion variability.
  • Modelled H₂O and OH emission using JWST/MIRI spectroscopy, finding temperatures and column densities consistent with emission originating within the inner ≲1 AU of the disk.
  • Combined CO and H₂O diagnostics indicate a warm, molecule-rich inner disk exhibiting only modest variability between the observing epochs.
